\hypertarget{population-dynamics}{%
\subsubsection*{Population Dynamics}\label{population-dynamics}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{Population Dynamics}

For population \(s\), the number-at-age \(a\) that survived
(\(N_{S,s,a,t+\delta}\)), were harvested from the fishery
(\(N_{H,s,a,t+\delta}\)), and died from natural mortality
(\(N_{D,s,a,t+\delta}\)) over the time interval \(\delta\) are given by:

\setcounter{equation}{0}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{S,s,a,t+\delta} &= N_{s,a,t} S(s,a,\delta) = N_{s,a,t} e^{-Z_{s,a}\delta} \\
N_{H,s,a,t+\delta} &= N_{s,a,t} H(s,a,\delta) = N_{s,a,t} \frac{F_{s,a}}{Z_{s,a}}\left(1 - e^{-Z_{s,a}\delta}\right) \\
N_{D,s,a,t+\delta} &= N_{s,a,t} D(s,a,\delta) = N_{s,a,t} \frac{M_{s,a}}{Z_{s,a}}\left(1 - e^{-Z_{s,a}\delta}\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

where \(N_{s,a,t}\) is the number-at-age \(a\) at time \(t\) for
population \(s\); \(S\), \(H\), and \(D\) represent the proportions
(fractions) of individuals that survived, were harvested, or died from
natural mortality, respectively, over the time interval \(\delta\);
\(F_{t,a}\) and \(M_{t,a}\) denote instantaneous rates of fishing
mortality and natural mortality at age \(a\) and time \(t\), and
\(Z_{s,a}\) denotes total mortality rate, where
\(Z_{s,a} = F_{s,a} + M_{s,a}\).

The probability transition matrix, when there is movement between
regions, can be generalized as:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_{s,a,t,\delta} = \begin{bmatrix}
 \mathbf{O}(s,a,\delta) & \mathbf{H}(s,a,\delta) & \mathbf{D}(s,a,\delta) \\
 0 & \mathbf{I}_{H} & 0 \\
 0 & 0 & \mathbf{I}_{D}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{O}\) is the \(n_R \times n_R\) matrix representing the
combined probability of survival and movement between regions. Assuming
movement occurs sequentially after survival, \(\mathbf{O}\) is the
product of the survival probability matrix (\(\mathbf{S}\)) and movement
probability matrix \(\mu\), such that \(\mathbf{O} = \mathbf{S} \mu\).
\(\mathbf{H}\) is the \(n_R \times n_F\) matrix representing proportions
of individuals harvested by each fleet in each region, \(\mathbf{D}\) is
the \(n_R \times n_R\) matrix representing proportions of individuals
that died from natural mortality within each region, and
\(\mathbf{I}_{H}\) and \(\mathbf{I}_{D}\), which have the same
dimensions as \(\mathbf{H}\) and \(\mathbf{D}\)), are identity matrices
that ensure the overall dimensions of the probability transition matrix
remain consistent when combined. If there is only one region and one
fleet, these identity matrices reduce to scalars of 1.

For population \(s\), the total number-at-age \(a\) at the end of time
interval \(\delta\) can be generalized as:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{N}_{s,a,t+\delta} = \mathbf{P}(s,a,\delta)' \mathbf{N}_{s,a,t}
\end{equation}

where the numbers of individuals that survived, were harvested, and died
from natural mortality are decomposed as:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{N}_{S,s,a,t+\delta} &= \mathbf{O}(s,a,\delta)' \mathbf{N}_{s,a,t} \\
\mathbf{N}_{H,s,a,t+\delta} &= \mathbf{H}(s,a,\delta)' \mathbf{N}_{s,a,t} \\
\mathbf{N}_{D,s,a,t+\delta} &= \mathbf{D}(s,a,\delta)' \mathbf{N}_{s,a,t}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\hypertarget{population-structure-and-seasonality}{%
\subsubsection*{Population Structure and
Seasonality}\label{population-structure-and-seasonality}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{Population Structure and
Seasonality}

A natal homing population structure is assumed in WHAM. Each population
is associated with a natal region, and all fish originating in that
region must return there for spawning. Outside of the spawning season,
fish can move between regions (see details in the sections on OM or EM
movement dynamics). Seasonality is included to support the assumption of
natal homing. Specifically, a number of time intervals within a year
(\(\Sigma_{i=1}^{n} \delta_i = 1\)) are used to indicate movement
dynamics prior to the spawning season, during the spawning season, and
after the spawning season. The probability transition matrix for the
entire year is a product of all ``seasonal'' matrices:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_{s,a,y} = \prod^{n}_{t=1} \mathbf{P}_{s,a,y,t}
\end{equation}

\hypertarget{fishing-mortality-f}{%
\subsubsection*{\texorpdfstring{Fishing Mortality
(\(F\))}{Fishing Mortality (F)}}\label{fishing-mortality-f}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{Fishing Mortality (\(F\))}

\({F}\) at age \({a}\) for fleet \({f}\) is calculated as a product of
fully selected \({F}\) and selectivity-at-age (i.e., the separability
assumption) for fleet \({f}\):
\(\tilde F_{f,a} = \tilde F \textit{Sel}_{f,a}\). In WHAM, a fully
selected, total \({F}\) is the maximum of the total \({F}\)-at-age
summed across all fleets:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_{\text{total},a,y} &= \sum_{i=1}^{n_f} F_{f_i,a,y} \\
F_{\text{total},y} &= \operatorname*{argmax}_a F_{\text{total},a,y} \\
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\hypertarget{reference-points}{%
\subsubsection*{Reference Points}\label{reference-points}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{Reference Points}

The fully selected total \(F\) defined above was used to estimate a
single universal \({F}\) reference point applicable to all populations
and regions. A `static' SPR-based reference point was calculated by
averaging all of the inputs (e.g., selectivity, natural mortality,
movement, weight-at-age, maturity-at-age) over the most recent 5 model
years for each region, except for recruitment, which was averaged over
all model years for each region.

For \(X\%\) SPR-based reference points, WHAM uses a Newton method to
solve for the universal \(F_{X\%}\) reference point:

\begin{equation}
\tilde{F}^{(i)} = \tilde{F}^{(i-1)} - \frac{g\left(\tilde{F}^{(i-1)}\right)}{\tilde{F}^{(i-1)}g'\left(\tilde{F}^{(i-1)}\right)}
\end{equation}

where \(g(F)\) is the difference between the weighted spawning biomass
per recruit at \(F\) and \(X\%\) of the weighted unfished spawning
biomass per recruit:

\begin{equation}
g(F) = \sum_{s=1}^S w_s \left( \frac{\tilde{\tilde{SSB}}}{\tilde{R}(F)} - \frac{X}{100} \frac{\tilde{\tilde{SSB}}}{\tilde{R}(F=0)} \right)
\end{equation}

Here weights are determined by the average recruitment for different
regions/populations:

\begin{equation}
w_i = \frac{\bar{R}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n_s} \bar{R}_i}
\end{equation}

The fully selected total \(F\) reference point can be disaggregated to
regional \(F\) reference points, given the relative contribution of
selectivity (\(Sel\)) at age \(a\) from each fleet in each region \(R\):

\begin{equation}
F_{X\%_{\text{a},R}} = F_{X\%} \text{$Sel$}_{a,R}
\end{equation}

The regional \(F_{X\%}\) (e.g., \(F_{40\%}\)) does not guarantee
achieving 40\% of region-specific SPR, but serve to collectively achieve
40\% of SPR at the global scale. Regional \(F\) reference points are not
currently implemented, as a unique solution is not guaranteed in the
presence of movement between regions. An analogous Newton method is used
to solve for \(F\) that maximizes yield for MSY-based reference points.
